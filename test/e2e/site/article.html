Why sourdough starters fail

A sourdough starter fails most often because of temperature. Wild yeast and lactobacillus bacteria need warmth between 24 and 28 degrees. Below that the culture goes dormant.

Hydration

Hydration of the starter matters: a stiff starter ferments slower than a liquid levain. Many bakers in San Francisco keep a 100% hydration levain.

Flour choice

Rye flour feeds the culture faster than white flour because it carries more amylase enzymes and wild yeast. Chad Robertson of Tartine Bakery recommends a rye and whole wheat blend.
